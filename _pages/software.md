I have been an active developer of the [Sedona Radiative Transfer Code](https://github.com/dnkasen/pubsed) where I implemented quasi-NLTE capabilities for kilonovae and zone-by-zone variations in r-process heating thermalization efficiency (see [Publications](publications) page for more details).

While the purpose of QNLTE and thermalization efficiency was designed for simulating kilonovae, there are applications to other transient phenomena as well. Many supernovae are powered by Ni56, which behave in a very similar manner. Tidal Disruption Events (TDEs), FBOTs, and collapsars could also be influnced by QNLTE through varying photoionization rates and radioactive decay. 

I have also developed my own publically available tools for post-processing Sedona spectra, which can be found [here](https://github.com/dbrethauer/SedonaTools_DB).
