Massive stars end their lives in the spectacular cosmic fireworks display known as a supernova. But some, like SN2014C, are quite strange. Initially, when the supernova first went off, it showed no signs of containing hydrogen and was considered to be quite ordinary. However, approximately 100 days later suddenly there were booming signs of hydrogen! This indicated that the star that produced the explosion had likely been almost entirely stripped of its outer layers of hydrogen and sprayed the material into its surroundings thousands of years prior to explosion. Then, once the explosion occurred and shrapnel started flying out, it slammed into the hydrogen causing the abundance of hydrogen signals observed. 

Our analysis of the X-ray data indicated that this was likely due to a binary companion siphoning off the outer layers of the star in the final millenia of the massive star's life, and we were witnessing a cosmic crime scene!
